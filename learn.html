Before choosing a route

“Hard” is more than one number.

High Colorado separates terrain class, physical effort, exposure, rockfall, route-finding, commitment and trailhead access. A long Class 1 hike and a short exposed Class 3 scramble can demand completely different skills.

🥾

Class 1

Trail hiking. Physical effort can still be substantial because of distance, gain, altitude and weather.

🪨

Class 2

Rougher hiking, talus or off-trail movement. Hands may occasionally help with balance.

👐

Class 3

Scrambling where hands are used for upward movement. Exposure and route-finding often matter much more.

🧗

Class 4

Steeper climbing with serious consequences. Experience, judgment and route-specific preparation are essential.

⚠ExposureWhat are the consequences of a fall?
🪨RockfallHow unstable or loose is the terrain?
🧭Route-findingHow easy is it to stay on the intended line?

The key planning rule

Choose a route, not just a peak

The same mountain can offer very different experiences depending on the route. High Colorado should recommend a route based on your experience, available time, vehicle, terrain comfort and conditions rather than labeling an entire mountain “easy” or “hard.”
